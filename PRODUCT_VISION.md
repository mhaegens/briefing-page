# Briefing Hub — personal presales operating system

## North star

Open the hub and know what deserves attention in under ten seconds.

Briefing Hub should not become another dashboard to maintain. It is the calm decision layer between Mitchell, his Obsidian knowledge, customer work, and a crew of agents. Agents gather and prepare; Mitchell supplies judgment, relationships, and final approval.

## The core loop

1. **Capture** — a thought, email, meeting, document, or customer signal enters the hub.
2. **Add context** — the hub links it to an opportunity, person, meeting, and relevant Obsidian notes.
3. **Decide** — only the smallest useful decision is shown, one card at a time.
4. **Delegate** — an agent receives a bounded action with the context and evidence it needs.
5. **Return** — the result comes back as a curated briefing, not a chat transcript.
6. **Remember** — durable knowledge is promoted into Obsidian; disposable working content is purged.

## Primary navigation

Keep the permanent navigation small:

- **Today** — the current focus, next meeting, and resumable work.
- **Work** — opportunity and customer workspaces.
- **Knowledge** — Obsidian search, evidence, and reusable assets.
- **Agents** — delegated work, blocked jobs, and completed results.
- **History** — decisions and content-free audit records.

“Briefing” is a mode used inside every section rather than a permanent destination. The current Inbox can evolve into Today.

## Focus Beacon

The top-right diamond is the **Focus Beacon**, not a notification centre.

It may interrupt only for:

- a customer commitment or deadline at risk;
- an agent blocked on a human decision;
- a factual, legal, commercial, or architectural risk;
- a meeting starting soon that is not prepared;
- work the user explicitly asked to be alerted about.

Every beacon item must contain:

- why it matters now;
- the smallest next decision;
- an honest effort estimate;
- one primary action and a quiet “later” route.

Routine completions, FYIs, sync events, and low-confidence suggestions remain in the inbox. No streaks, red badge inflation, or endless feed.

## High-value presales modules

### Opportunity cockpit

A large callout shows the single biggest deal risk. Supporting bites cover stage, next customer commitment, stakeholder map, unanswered questions, competitors, required assets, and a timeline of recent signals. Agents can monitor CRM, email, meeting notes, and proposal changes.

### Meeting runway

Before a meeting: objective, people, relationship history, open promises, likely objections, three questions to ask, and relevant Obsidian notes. After the meeting: decisions, commitments, follow-ups, CRM update, and candidate knowledge to keep.

### RFP and proposal workbench

Requirement coverage, evidence links, contradictions, unsupported claims, owner, deadline, and a “rewrite with approved evidence” action. Every generated claim should retain source lineage and a confidence state.

### Demo planner

Map customer pains to demo moments. Generate a narrative arc, environment checklist, roles, talk track, expected objections, and recovery paths. Preserve reusable demo patterns in Obsidian.

### People memory

A respectful working memory for stakeholders: role, priorities, communication preferences, past decisions, promises made, and the next useful move. Avoid speculative personality scoring.

### Knowledge desk

Ask across the Obsidian vault and active work, with note-level citations. Useful actions include “turn this into a customer answer,” “find prior examples,” “show conflicting notes,” and “save this result back to Obsidian.”

### Agent desk

Show four states only: **working**, **needs you**, **ready to review**, and **failed**. Each job has an owner, purpose, source scope, expected output, and stop condition. Raw agent chatter stays hidden unless troubleshooting is requested.

### Capture dock

A universal text or voice capture available from every screen. The user records the thought first; an agent proposes the opportunity, person, task, or Obsidian note it belongs to. Nothing is auto-filed without a reversible review step.

## ADHD interaction rules

- One dominant action per screen; at most three visible choices when possible.
- Show “why now,” estimated effort, and a clear finish line.
- Preserve a **resume capsule**: what was being done, where it stopped, and the next move.
- Offer energy-sized views such as **2 minutes**, **15 minutes**, and **deep work**.
- Hide completed and routine material by default, while keeping it findable.
- Use progressive disclosure: headline, callout, bullets, visual evidence, then source detail.
- Make snoozing explicit and shame-free. A deferred item returns with its context intact.
- Celebrate completion briefly, then restore calm; avoid streaks and gamified pressure.
- Never put more than three items in the interruption layer.

## Memory and privacy boundaries

Use three different memory layers:

1. **Durable knowledge — Obsidian.** Curated facts, reusable methods, customer-approved knowledge, templates, and personal notes.
2. **Operational state — hub database.** Status, assignments, deadlines, source references, decisions, and resume position.
3. **Ephemeral content — briefings.** Raw mail excerpts, generated analysis, temporary attachments, and working context that can be purged after review.

Agents may propose an Obsidian update, but Mitchell approves what becomes durable knowledge. Every factual card should link back to its evidence and show when that evidence was last verified.

## Suggested agent crew

- **Morning Scout** — prepares the day and highlights only genuine conflicts or risks.
- **Meeting Prep Agent** — builds pre-reads and post-meeting follow-ups.
- **RFP Auditor** — checks requirements, claims, evidence, and contradictions.
- **Deal Coach** — spots stakeholder gaps, stalled commitments, and next-best actions.
- **Demo Planner** — turns pains and requirements into a tailored demonstration.
- **Knowledge Gardener** — finds duplicate, stale, orphaned, or newly reusable Obsidian notes.
- **Follow-up Drafter** — prepares emails and tasks, always waiting for approval before sending.

## Recommended delivery order

### First useful hub

1. Focus Beacon with strict interruption rules.
2. Today page with current focus, next meeting, and resume capsule.
3. Obsidian search with citations and approved save-back.
4. Agent desk with working / needs you / ready / failed states.
5. Universal capture dock.

### Presales expansion

6. Meeting runway.
7. Opportunity cockpit.
8. RFP and proposal workbench.
9. Demo planner and reusable asset library.

### Later automation

10. Calendar, email, CRM, and collaboration connectors.
11. Proactive monitoring with user-controlled alert policies.
12. Cross-opportunity insights and personal rhythm suggestions.

The product succeeds when it reduces context switching and forgotten commitments—not when it displays the most data.
